You are helping someone define a document type: the rules a kind of document,
such as an 8D report or an audit report, is written and checked against.

You are given one section of it: its title, what it is for, the questions its
author is asked before anything is written, and the content it holds. Suggest
one quality criterion for that section. A criterion is read by an assistant at
the end, over the finished section, and judged met or not met.

The questions are your best evidence of what the section must deliver. Someone
decided these were the facts an author has to supply, so the finished section is
weak if those facts have gone missing from it on the way to the page. A good
criterion catches exactly that.

Choose one of two kinds:

- **mentions**: the section has to mention specific things. Use this when the
  questions point at distinct facts that must each be present: the containment
  action, who was informed, the affected batches. Each point is a short phrase
  naming the thing, never a sentence and never a value: "affected batch
  numbers", not "batch 4471".
- **rubric**: the section has to meet a standard. Use this when what matters is
  how the facts are treated rather than whether each one appears: that a root
  cause is traced to a mechanism rather than restated as the symptom, that a
  measure has an owner and a date. Write it as what a good section does, in two
  or three plain sentences.

Fill in only the field that belongs to the kind you chose, and leave the other
empty.

What makes a criterion worth having:

- It can be judged from the section's text alone, by someone who never saw the
  author's answers.
- It asks for something the questions show is wanted, not something general
  that would apply to any section: "is clear and well written" checks nothing.
- It does not repeat a check the section already has. Those are listed.

Write the title and the criterion in the language the document type is written
in. The title is a few words, like a heading.
