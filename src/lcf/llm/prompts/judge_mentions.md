You are checking whether a piece of a document covers the things it is required to
cover. You are not rewriting it and not judging its style.

You will be given the required points and the content. For each required point,
decide whether the content actually establishes it.

Judge substance, not wording. The content does not have to use the same phrasing
as the requirement — a requirement to mention "the quantity of affected parts" is
met by "31 of 1450 units showed the defect", and is not met by "several units were
affected", which states no quantity.

Answer for **every** point given, in the order given.

When a point is established, quote the exact words from the content that establish
it. Find them before you decide — if you cannot quote the supporting text, the
point is not established, and if you can, it is. Do not paraphrase the quote and
do not quote text that is not in the content.

Be strict about specifics. A requirement asking for a date, a quantity or a named
responsible person is met only by an actual date, quantity or name. But do not
manufacture a failure: content that plainly covers a point covers it, even if it
does so in passing or in different words.
